5 INSPIRING WEB ARTISTS

Yoshimoto Nara

Yoshimoto Nara's work portrays portraits of cartoon-like/childlike figures . While his work is widely percieved as cute, there are deeper meanings to his work, often implementing themes such as rebellion, non-conformity, and loneliness. I lean more towards art/artists that are categorized as cute, but explore themes that are opposite to the works' appearance.

Cybertwee

I like this artist’s page because it feels like a softer, more emotional version of tech culture. It mixes digital elements with something cute, dreamy, and almost nostalgic. The whole page feels like a moodboard where fantasy and real life blur together, which makes it feel really immersive and personal.

Teo Spiller

I like Teo Spiller’s work because it treats the internet itself as the artwork, not just a place to post things. His pieces feel really raw and interactive, almost like you’re moving through a space instead of just looking at it. There’s something interesting about how simple and early-web it feels, but still intentional; it makes the experience more about exploration than visuals. It shows that websites can feel like environments, not just pages.

Cory Arcangel

I like his work because it takes really familiar digital things like old games or YouTube aesthetics, and turns them into something slightly off or recontextualized. It feels simple at first, but there's something subtle about how it makes you think about how we consume media online. It's not overly polished, which makes it feel more real and internet-native.

Morehshin Allahyari

I like Allahyari's work because it feels more conceptual but still really tied to digital space. She uses technology to tell stories about identity and history, which makes the work feel deeper than just visuals. It’s interesting how she uses digital tools not just aesthetically, but as a way to reconstruct or imagine different realities.
